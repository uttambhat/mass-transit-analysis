# Bay Area Mass Transit Datasets

*Proposal research brief — October 2026*

## Purpose and feasible research question

A strong funding case can quantify where transit demand, service quality, and roadway use move together, while avoiding a causal claim that a drop in transit ridership *caused* more driving. A practical first study is: **for monthly or quarterly origin–destination (OD) pairs, are larger declines in transit trips associated with increases in roadway vehicle-miles traveled (VMT), congestion, or car commute share after accounting for remote work, jobs, population, fuel prices, seasonality, and service supplied?** The appropriate unit is an anonymized zone-to-zone pair (e.g., census tract, Traffic Analysis Zone, or BART-station catchment) by time period.

No single public dataset identifies all Bay Area trips. The most credible approach combines operator ridership and schedules, regional travel surveys, roadway data, and a limited member-collected panel.

## Highest-value existing datasets

| Dataset | What it supplies | Fit, access, and caveats |
|---|---|---|
| [BART Ridership Reports](https://www.bart.gov/about/reports/ridership) | Monthly snapshots, historical actual-ridership workbooks, and station-pair/OD material; BART publishes current monthly reports and historical series. | Best public source for rail OD changes. Map stations to fixed catchment zones, retain the agency's month/date fields, and use a pre-pandemic baseline plus post-pandemic panel. It excludes bus, walk, bike, and non-BART trips; a station-to-station trip is not a neighborhood-to-neighborhood trip. |
| [SFMTA Muni Ridership](https://data.sfgov.org/Transportation/Muni-Ridership/mqyf-7b6w) | Route/line ridership and historical trend data for San Francisco's bus, rail, cable-car, and historic-streetcar service. | Use to measure within-SF service and ridership, particularly where BART is not representative. Boardings are not unique passengers and generally do not produce a complete passenger OD matrix. |
| [Caltrain reports and statistics](https://www.caltrain.com/about-caltrain/reports-statistics) and [AC Transit data/statistics](https://www.actransit.org/data-stats) | Agency performance, ridership, and service material for Peninsula rail and East Bay buses. | Necessary companions to BART/Muni for multi-county coverage. Confirm exact time frequency, route/station granularity, revisions, and licensing before a regional panel; retain agency-specific definitions rather than forcing incomparable counts together. |
| [511 Transit Open Data](https://511.org/open-data/transit) | Regional static GTFS, GTFS-Realtime vehicle positions/trip updates/service alerts, and historical feed access for participating agencies (free registration/API key may be required). | Reconstruct service supplied: scheduled trips, headways, span, stop access, cancellations, and observed vehicle movement. It is not ridership; use it to distinguish demand loss from reduced service. Archive feeds because schedules change. |
| [MTC Bay Area Travel Study (BATS)](https://mtc.ca.gov/bay-area-travel-study) and [2019/2023 dashboard](https://bats2019-2023.mtcanalytics.org/) | Household travel diaries: trip origin/destination, mode, purpose, timing, and household/traveler characteristics. MTC's detailed microdata may require an approved public-agency/university request and NDA. | Best regional benchmark for all modes and trip purpose, including auto and transit. Survey weights and sampling uncertainty matter; it is periodic, not a monthly traffic counter. Use public summaries first and budget time for controlled-access microdata. |
| [SFMTA Travel Decision Survey 2021](https://data.sf.gov/Transportation/Travel-Decision-Survey-2021/b7eg-vqw2) | Open downloadable data on trips into, out of, and within San Francisco: mode, purpose, demographics, and OD-related travel information. Historical surveys are also available. | A public, SF-focused complement to BATS for mode shifts. It does not represent the whole nine-county region equally, so do not generalize its estimates to all Bay Area neighborhoods. |
| [Census LEHD LODES](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/data/#lodes), [LED extraction tool](https://ledextract.ces.census.gov/lodes/od), and [OnTheMap](https://onthemap.ces.census.gov/) | Annual census-block residence-to-workplace flows plus worker/job characteristics; downloadable California OD files or custom extracts. | Provides a consistent pre/post-pandemic **commute** OD denominator and identifies corridors where transit investment could affect access to jobs. It does not report travel mode, all-purpose travel, daily traffic, or individual routes. |
| [MTC/ABAG Open Data](https://opendata.mtc.ca.gov/) and [Vital Signs daily miles traveled](https://vitalsigns.mtc.ca.gov/indicators/daily-miles-traveled) | Regional boundaries, network/planning layers, demographic context, and VMT indicators/downloads. The [BAAQMD/MTC VMT portal](https://www.baaqmd.gov/en/plans-and-climate/local-government-support/vehicle-miles-traveled-data-portal) adds planning/GHG-oriented VMT resources. | Use for regional/city/county trend context and zone definitions. Confirm geography, modeled versus observed status, road classes, and vintage; aggregate VMT cannot by itself be assigned to an OD pair. |
| [Caltrans PeMS](https://pems.dot.ca.gov/) and [Caltrans PeMS description](https://dot.ca.gov/programs/traffic-operations/mpr/pems-source) | Detector-level freeway flow, speed, and occupancy, commonly at five-minute intervals; registration is required. | Best source for freeway congestion and directional traffic near transit corridors. Convert detector counts/speeds into corridor indicators; it is not a complete local-street count or a direct measure of vehicle distance without network assumptions. |
| [National Transit Database](https://www.transit.dot.gov/ntd/data-product/monthly-module-adjusted-data-release) | Standardized agency monthly service and ridership measures, including unlinked passenger trips and vehicle-revenue metrics. | Useful for comparable trends across operators and a transparent cross-check of agency reports. It lacks local passenger OD detail and agencies' reporting definitions/timing can differ. |

## Recommended analysis design

1. **Create a corridor/OD panel.** Start with BART station-pairs and station catchments; add Muni/AC Transit/Caltrain route or stop catchments only where their data support a defensible geography. Join monthly ridership to scheduled/observed service from GTFS, nearby PeMS corridor flow/speed, and VMT trends. Use BATS, LODES, and the SFMTA survey for calibration and demographic/commute context—not as false high-frequency counts.
2. **Measure changes, not raw levels.** Compare equivalent month/weekday periods to a stated baseline; report passenger trips, service supplied, congestion/VMT proxy, and uncertainty. Model fixed effects for OD pair and time, with controls for employment/residential change, remote-work prevalence where available, gas price, weather, construction/disruptions, and special events. Test leads/lags: a contemporaneous correlation is not displacement.
3. **Frame funding scenarios.** Estimate the association under observed high- and low-service/reliability periods, then describe projected ridership, accessibility, travel-time reliability, VMT, and emissions as scenarios with ranges. Do not claim each recovered transit trip removes one car trip; occupancy, induced demand, transfers, and access mode vary.
4. **Publish reproducibly.** Release only aggregate zone-period measures, documented geographic crosswalks, versioned GTFS snapshots, code, data dictionary, and a limitation statement. Obtain permission for any controlled BATS/Clipper-derived data and never publish identifiable travel traces.

## Crowdsourcing plan for approximately 200 nonprofit members

The member panel cannot estimate regional ridership on its own, but it can capture the reasons behind mode choice and validate gaps in administrative records. Recruit a stratified volunteer panel by county, transit dependence, income/age/disability status where participants choose to disclose, and typical mode; compare the panel's composition to BATS/Census benchmarks and weight or clearly label it as a convenience sample.

**Low-burden recurring diary (recommended):** ask members for one assigned weekday and one weekend day each quarter, plus optional two-week waves around service changes. For each trip collect coarse origin/destination zone (not address), departure/arrival window, primary and access/egress modes, route/line if applicable, transfer count, party size, trip purpose, fare/parking/toll cost ranges, planned versus actual travel time, cancellation/overcrowding/safety/accessibility issue, and counterfactual (“if this transit trip were unavailable, what would you do?”). Permit “prefer not to say,” retain only zone/time bins, and delete raw location data after aggregation.

**Targeted gap modules:** (a) first/last-mile barriers and sidewalk/bike/ADA access; (b) reliability and missed connections, corroborated against GTFS-Realtime; (c) trips that agency fare data miss—walk, bike, carpool, ride-hail, non-Clipper fares, and abandoned transit attempts; (d) employer commute policies and remote-work days; and (e) qualitative evidence of destinations/jobs/services made inaccessible by poor frequency or late-night service.

**Privacy and data quality:** do not request account numbers, payment-card/fare-card identifiers, exact home/work address, continuous GPS traces, passenger names, or employer-confidential information. Obtain informed consent, publish retention/access rules, encrypt raw survey exports, restrict access to the research team, and suppress small cells (for example, fewer than 10 responses). Use validation checks for impossible times/distances, duplicate entries, and route plausibility; treat discrepancies as findings to investigate, not records to silently overwrite.

## Immediate next steps

Begin with a two-corridor pilot: one BART-heavy transbay or East Bay–SF pair and one bus-heavy neighborhood pair. Inventory exact fields and historical coverage before modeling, request BATS access if eligible, register for 511/PeMS, and pre-register the outcome definitions. A pilot can establish whether public data plus 200 members are sufficient for an evidence-backed funding narrative before expanding regionwide.
